# Looper for HiChord

An unofficial fan project, not made by or affiliated with Pocket Audio, the makers of the HiChord.

A looper for the HiChord pocket synth, in the spirit of [fm1-sampler](https://github.com/ferluht/fm1-sampler): you play on the HiChord, this app records it in layers and makes the sound, and you change timing, instruments and mix while it loops.

Open it at https://a0ies.github.io/hichord-sampler/ (Chrome or Edge), or use the iPhone app in `ios/`.

## How it works

- **Layers.** Eight layers; whatever you play goes into the selected one. “+ new layer” sits below the list, and as a small line of text right above the first layer: pull the list down at its top and the text grows into a key; scroll down and it shrinks back; once the key at the bottom is in view, the one on top steps aside. Press record (R), play, press record again. Each pass is a take you can undo.
- **Capture mode.** Capture (C) turns every layer into a full-screen touch pad, with its icon and a small timeline. Tap a pad to switch the layer on or off; hold and drag to play its effect (low-pass, high-pass, stutter gate, delay throw, reverb wash, pitch bend or drive, set per layer under Mix, or by tapping the effect label next to the layer's name). A pad can play several effects at once: under Mix, “effects” holds a chip per effect; tap one to set its range (how far it goes at the top of the pad) or remove it, and “+” adds another. One drag plays them all together. Let go and the effect springs back, unless you lock it: hold the effect still for 2 seconds (a ring fills around your finger) and the lock key in the bottom-left corner (a red lock, above undo) starts to pulse. Press it with your other hand and the effect stays where you left it; the key shows a closed lock with the layer's number, and pressing it again releases the effect. Touch a locked pad to move its effect. At the bottom, a record strip (in place of the transport bar) records what you hear to an audio file, with a timer and level meter; capture mode never records into a layer.
- **Layer effects.** On the layers screen, each layer has effect chips (low-pass, high-pass, gate, delay, reverb, pitch, drive): “+” adds one, tap a chip to set its amount or remove it. They stay on; capture mode plays on top of them and springs back to them. The gate has note keys (1/4 to 1/32, and triplets) in its pop-up and on its capture pad; in capture mode dragging up makes it deeper.
- **MIDI, not audio.** The app stores the notes you played. That is why timing and instruments stay editable after recording.
- **Timing, live.** Per layer: quantize (1/4 to 1/16 triplets), strength, swing, nudge, note length, humanize, octave. Switch from feel to advanced for an XY screen that sets strength (left–right) and humanize (up–down), with the grid, swing, note length and nudge underneath. The layer panel keeps one height on every page, with clear notes and remove layer (tap twice) at the bottom, and above it a preview screen: the layer's timeline with its playhead, and under it a strip with the live waveform, filter curve and envelope, a play key and a solo key. Your original timing is never changed; Quantize Off brings it back. Tempo changes re-time every layer at once.
- **Instruments.** Grand piano (Salamander), strings and horns (VSCO 2 CE), electric guitar (FreePats), electric bass (Karoryfer Growlybass) and drums (Virtuosity Drums), all openly licensed; electric piano, saw lead, square lead, pad and synth bass are synthesised for this app. Sources and licences are in CREDITS.md and under Settings → Credits. Each lives in `sounds/` as one AAC file with a note every 3 semitones (or close to it); `sounds/sounds.json` holds where each note and its loop start, built by `tools/build_sounds.py`. Bass and drum layers play single notes: bass plays the chord's root, and on drums every chord key gets its own drum (I kick, II snare, III hat, IV open hat, V tom, VI percussion, VII crash, following the HiChord's key). The HiChord's drum pads on channel 10 can still hit two drums at once. Or use your own sound: an audio file (from Files) or the microphone.
- **HiChord instruments.** Settings → Sync, while the HiChord is connected: “sample the HiChord's sounds” opens a full screen. First the steps on the HiChord: firmware 3.0 (the version shows under the logo at start-up; older ones update on a computer in Chrome or Edge at updater.hichord.shop), the settings (press YELLOW, joystick ↑ UP for the effects, ← LEFT / → RIGHT to each row, ↑ UP to change it: reverb, delay, chorus and glide off, MIDI Out DAW, MIDI In on, USB Audio on), the volume wheel down to about 5% so nobody hears it, and USB-C. Then pick any of the HiChord's 24 melodic sounds: the 10 that stand in for the app's instruments (grand piano → Piano, electric piano → Tine EP, strings → Strings, horns → Brass, electric guitar → Acoustic, saw lead → Saw, square lead → Square, pad → Vox Ahh, electric bass → Triangle, synth bass → SawSquare; drums stay) and 14 more that become instruments of their own (Sine, FM EPiano, FM Wow, Clarinet, Vibraphone, Retro EP, Harp, Humming, Robbo, Shutter, Flute, Ocean, Buzz Org, Organ). The app switches the HiChord to each sound over USB MIDI, plays it a note every 3 semitones from C2 to C7 (about a minute per sound), records its USB audio, cuts the notes (timing plus onset detection, for USB latency), trims and levels them (a quiet HiChord is fine) and finds loop points; afterwards the HiChord goes back to its own sound. While it runs the screen shows the sound, its 21 notes filling up, the input and its level, the time left and every sound of the run; stop keeps what is done, and it stops with a hint if no sound comes in. Each sound is kept as soon as it is done: in the iPhone app as files in the app's own storage (they stay through app updates, until the app is deleted), in a browser in its storage (asked to be kept). Then every layer plays the HiChord instruments, named after them, and the 14 extra sounds are in the instrument lists. Two keys under Settings → Sync show where things stand, each orange with a check once done: “HiChord sounds sampled” (tap to sample again; while some are missing it says how many) and “playing HiChord instruments” (tap to go back to the open-source ones); and the HiChord key on a layer's instrument page switches just that layer (orange: HiChord, white: open source; not sampled yet: it samples that one). The samples are not part of a project; a shared project names its instruments, and the other device plays its own HiChord samples, or the closest open-source instrument.
- **Loop.** 1 to 16 bars, ×2 to double, count-in and click.
- **Song parts.** Above the layers: parts A, B, C… Each part has its own notes per layer and its own set of layers on or off; instruments, effects and mix are shared. Tap the part you are on (or the song key) for the song page: a preview of the part that plays, the whole song as one tape under a fixed playhead (one block is one pass of the loop), and the parts as pads. Drag a pad onto the tape to add a pass; hold a block and drag it to move it, or onto “remove”. Tap a block or pad to go there (while playing: after this loop); swipe the tape when stopped; hold a pad to name, copy or remove the part. “auto” plays the tape from part to part; off, the part you are on loops. Capture mode follows the same tape, with a countdown in the last bar.
- **Note editor (piano roll).** Opens with the select tool, on chords (bass and drums on single notes), and shows the whole loop again when you turn the phone. Undo and redo sit at the left of its toolbar (so nothing floats over the trim lane), close sits top right. Like in Ableton: double-click/double-tap to add, drag to move in time and pitch, drag an edge to lengthen or shorten, drag empty space to select, Alt-drag to copy, a velocity lane, snap from 1/4 to 1/32, quantize, duplicate, undo/redo and keyboard shortcuts (⌫, ⌘D, ⌘A, ⌘Z, arrows, Q, B). On a phone: one finger pans, two fingers zoom, Draw adds with a single tap.
- **HiChord.** Notes always come in over USB MIDI. Connect HiChord to also see and set its key, scale, octave, play mode and sound, follow its tempo or send yours, and let its start/stop run the loop.
- **Settings.** The HiChord's screen sits at the top (on the left in landscape): connect, and once connected tap key, scale, octave, mode or sound to change it on the HiChord. Tabs underneath switch between sync, sound check and room; nothing scrolls.
- **Projects.** Every session is a project that saves itself (IndexedDB), including your own sounds. A new project is named after its creation date and time ("11 Nov 2026 · 14:32") until you rename it; a name you type stays. Open the projects sheet from the project name at the top to rename, duplicate, start a new project (the “+ new project” tile above your projects), open, or swipe a project to the left to delete it: a badge lets you restore it for 5 seconds before it is gone for good.
- **Share a project.** “share” next to the project's name sends the project to another device, own sounds included (file, microphone, sampled HiChord): you get a six-character code like `K7Q-M2P` (no 0, 1, I, L or O, so it reads out loud). On the other device: projects → “receive with a code”, type it, and the project opens there as a new project. No accounts. The project goes through a small relay (`relay/`, see below) as one gzipped file (the project as JSON, own sounds as 16-bit audio; built-in instruments travel by name only), at most 30 MB; the receiving device deletes it from the relay as soon as it has it, and anything not picked up is gone after 24 hours. Until a relay is set up, “share” explains that sharing isn't available yet.

## iPhone app

The same app runs on iPad, as a studio. In landscape the layers are a sidebar on the left. The note editor of the selected layer is always open across the top, with the graph strip (waveform, filter, envelope, play and solo) under it. Below that, the settings (timing, instrument and mix behind tabs) sit next to the layer's capture pad: drag to play its effects, hold still to lock them. In portrait the layers sit on top, two per row, with the same windows under them. Each window has a full-screen key: the settings show timing, instrument and mix side by side, in landscape the capture pad sits at full height next to the note editor and graph strip (the editor the wider of the two), in portrait it takes the whole width under them, and the note editor fills the screen.

iOS browsers have no Web MIDI, so `ios/` holds a small app: a full-screen WebView with a CoreMIDI bridge (`MIDIBridge.swift` + `midi-shim.js`). It loads the published page and falls back to the bundled copy offline.

```
cd ios && xcodegen generate && open HiChordSampler.xcodeproj
```
The app icon ("Layers") lives in `icons/icon.svg`; `sh tools/make_icons.sh` makes the iOS icon, the home-screen web clip and the favicon from it.
Pick your team under Signing & Capabilities, plug in the iPhone and press Run.

## Share relay (for “share a project”)

GitHub Pages only serves files, so sharing needs a tiny server: `relay/worker.js`, a Cloudflare Worker that stores a shared project in R2 under its code for 24 hours. `POST /share` returns a code, `GET /share/<code>` returns the file, `DELETE /share/<code>` removes it. It allows 30 MB per project and, per IP address, 10 uploads and 30 lookups a minute, so codes can't be guessed (there are 887 million). Only the GitHub Pages site and the iOS app may call it (`ALLOWED_ORIGINS` in `relay/wrangler.toml`). An hourly job deletes what has expired.

Deploying it, once, with your own Cloudflare account (the free plan is enough):

1. Make an account at https://dash.cloudflare.com/sign-up, then open **R2** in the dashboard and enable it (Cloudflare may ask for a payment method even for R2's free tier).
2. In this repository:
   ```
   cd relay
   npm install
   npx wrangler login
   npx wrangler r2 bucket create looper-shares
   npx wrangler deploy
   ```
   `wrangler login` opens the browser to allow access; `deploy` prints the relay's address, like `https://looper-relay.<your-subdomain>.workers.dev`. Opening it shows “Looper for HiChord share relay”.
3. Put that address in `index.html`: `const RELAY_URL = 'https://looper-relay.<your-subdomain>.workers.dev';`, bump `PAGE_VERSION`, commit and push. Share and receive work from then on.

If the site moves to another address, add it to `ALLOWED_ORIGINS` and run `npx wrangler deploy` again.

Testing locally, without an account: `cd relay && npm install && npm run dev` runs the relay at http://localhost:8787 with a local bucket. Serve the app at http://localhost:8417 and, in the browser console, `localStorage.setItem('hcl-relay','http://localhost:8787')` (remove it again with `localStorage.removeItem('hcl-relay')`).
